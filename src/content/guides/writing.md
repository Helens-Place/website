## The bit people miss

Writing gets treated as a basic skill that should simply be there by a certain
age. It is not basic. It is one of the most cognitively demanding things we ask
children to do, and we usually ask them to do it while also thinking about
something else.

That is why a child can talk fluently about a topic and then produce three
laboured sentences about it. The knowledge is not missing. The route from the
knowledge to the page is blocked.

## For teachers

Watch for the gap between what a student can tell you and what they hand in. If
it is wide, the problem is rarely comprehension or effort.

Scribing, typing, voice notes and mind maps are not ways of letting someone off.
They are ways of finding out what they know.

## For parents

Fine motor work does not have to look like schoolwork. Threading, model
building, cooking, Lego, scrunchie-making. All of it builds the hand strength and
control that handwriting rests on, and none of it feels like a chore.

If writing has become a nightly battle, that is worth taking seriously in its
own right. The avoidance is usually protective, not defiant.
