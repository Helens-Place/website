  - title: Give them space on the page
    body: >-
      Do not hand someone great bunched-up blocks of text. Generous line spacing
      and wider margins are the single most pragmatic change you can make, and
      they cost nothing. It sounds trivial. It is not.

    body: >-
      Fingers, rulers, a card under the line, a coloured overlay if it helps.

    body: >-

## Why this happens

Reading looks like one skill but it is several running at once: recognising
letter patterns, turning them into sounds, holding those sounds in order long
enough to blend them, and then holding the resulting sentence in mind long
enough to understand it.

Working memory carries a lot of that load. If it is unreliable, the sentence
falls apart before it is finished, and you go back to the start of the line.
That is exhausting, and it is why a slow reader is often a tired reader rather
than a lazy one.

## For teachers

The classroom changes that help most are the boring ones: spacing, not reading
round the class, giving processing time after a question, and never assuming
that a child who cannot read a text has not understood the idea in it.

Durrington Research School has genuinely good material on literacy in secondary
settings, and the work they do is worth your time.

## For parents

You do not need to become a reading teacher. What helps at home is keeping
reading pleasant: reading to them well past the age you think you should stop,
letting audiobooks count, and not turning the nightly reading record into a
battle.

If the difficulty is persistent, and particularly if there is a gap between how
articulate your child is out loud and what they manage on the page, that is the
point at which a proper assessment answers the question rather than guessing at
it.
