## What is actually happening

Getting an idea from your head onto paper involves pulling information out of
long term memory, retrieving it, getting it into an ordered space in working
memory, and then turning fairly abstract concepts into words and sentences that
can be written down.

That places an enormous load on executive function. When any part of that chain
is unreliable, the output bears no relation to the understanding behind it.

So the gap is not a puzzle. It is what you would predict.

## Why it matters that you noticed

This discrepancy is the point at which parents and teachers usually start
looking for more information, and they are right to. A child who is obviously
capable and consistently underperforms on paper is not lazy or unmotivated, and
being told that they are, year after year, does real damage.

The other reason it matters: these young people often work twice as hard for
half the credit, and know it. The confidence cost is frequently worse than the
academic one.

## What to do next

Talk to the school, and talk to each other. If the pattern is persistent, a
proper diagnostic assessment answers the question clearly rather than leaving
everyone guessing, and produces a report the school can act on.
