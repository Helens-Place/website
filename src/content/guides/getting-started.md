## Why it happens

Starting requires you to hold the whole task in mind, decide on an order, choose
an entry point, and begin, all at once. That leans heavily on working memory and
executive function, which are exactly the areas most often affected.

This is why the advice to just get started is useless. The starting is the
difficult part, not a preliminary to it.

## For teachers

Sentence starters and paragraph openers on the wall cost you one afternoon and
help every learner in the room, not just the dyslexic ones.

Watch for the student who has not written anything twelve minutes in. That is
not defiance, and asking what they are stuck on will usually get a precise
answer.

Nessy has good material on working memory aimed at Key Stage 2 and 3, which
covers a lot of the ground underneath this.

## For parents

Homework paralysis is worth naming out loud. Sitting with someone for the first
two minutes, helping them say the first sentence, and then leaving them to it, is
often the whole intervention.

If starting is a daily battle across every subject, that pattern is worth
mentioning when you talk to school, or to an assessor.
